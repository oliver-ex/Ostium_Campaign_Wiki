**Prepared for three level-1 PCs · D&D 5e (2024) · about 30–50 minutes.** Encounter for [[Storylines/Adventures/The Road to Ostium|The Road to Ostium]]. Three full-strength enemies and two minions threaten the caravan. All positions, numbers, tactics, and mechanical assignments below are **Planned** preparation, not completed events.

## Situation & Stakes

[[Characters/NPCs/Korok Grund|Korok Grund]] leads an ambush ordered by [[Factions/The Shattered Axe|The Shattered Axe]] against [[Characters/NPCs/Darian Auir|Darian Auir]]'s food, medical supplies, and gold. [[Characters/NPCs/Carl Marsh|Carl Marsh]] is among the attackers. Korok wants profitable cargo and surviving followers; Carl needs food for his wife and medicine for his daughter in Old Ostium. Her diagnosis remains unknown.

**GM-established setup for this planned encounter:** Korok and his gang impersonate city guards from [[Locations/New Ostium|New Ostium]]. They stop the caravan for a supposed inspection and claim authority to seize “contraband.” They are bandits using a false pretext; no genuine contraband, city inspection policy, or guard involvement is established.

**Opening prompt — Tentative wording:** “Broken stone narrows the road ahead. A branch lies across the wagon tracks. An orc in a poorly fitting guard coat raises a hand, his mace held low. Behind him, mismatched coats hang over worn leather armor; the others carry scimitars and crossbows. ‘New Ostium city guard. Halt for inspection. Open the wagon. Any contraband will be seized.’ One nervous man keeps glancing at the medicine crates.”

The PCs can protect the cargo, bargain over a share, turn back, scout a bypass, or fight. Make the claimed inspection, suspicious details, and escape route clear before initiative. Recognizing the fraud does not require the PCs to fight. There is no automatic surprise or free opening volley.

## Seeing Through the False Inspection

Give the PCs time to react before anyone boards the wagon or removes cargo. The following are **Tentative portrayal details** supporting the established impersonation:

- **Outfits:** ill-fitting, mismatched guard coats over patched leather; crude insignia attached unevenly, with one recruit wearing only an improvised armband. Describe these plainly rather than waiting for a Perception check.
- **Weaponry:** Korok's battered mace and the others' scimitars and crossbows look like an irregular collection alongside their attempted uniforms. The clue is the poor overall disguise, not a claim that real city guards could never carry those weapons. Disguises change no armor, attacks, or statistics.
- **Their “inspection”:** Korok labels ordinary food, medicine, and coin “contraband” without a coherent explanation, and directs followers to take it rather than identify an offence. Questions about his authority produce bluster or an attempt to hurry the search. Do not invent an actual prohibition to validate his story.
- **Darian's warning:** let the players notice and respond first. If they hesitate, accept the claim at face value, or miss the cues, Darian quietly points out the mismatched outfits and equipment **before they surrender cargo or allow themselves to be disarmed**. He offers a concrete observation, not an order to attack.

**Darian — Tentative dialogue:** “Look at those coats. None of them match, and their weapons are as mixed as their clothes. Calling my supplies contraband doesn't make them guards. This looks like a robbery.”

No roll is required to notice the visible discrepancies or hear Darian's warning. An optional **DC 10 Wisdom (Insight)** check can reveal that Korok is watching reactions and directing attention toward valuable cargo rather than conducting a methodical search; this is a proposed encounter ruling. Failure leaves the visible clues available. No failed check makes the disguise convincing by decree.

**If challenged:** Korok can insist the inspection is lawful, offer to overlook supposed contraband for a share, or drop the pretence and threaten robbery when exposed. The PCs can demand proof, refuse entry, keep talking, withdraw, feign cooperation, or fight. Calling the bluff does not automatically start combat. Let their choices determine whether the gang gets close to the wagon; do not award the bandits surprise merely because the PCs initially listened.

### Balance & Assumptions

The [official 2024 encounter budgets](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/br-2024/dms-toolbox#CombatEncounterDifficulty) for three level-1 PCs are **150 XP Low, 225 XP Moderate, and 300 XP High**. The three full-strength enemies total **150 XP**. Counting each minion conservatively at its unmodified Bandit value adds 50 XP: **200 XP total**, below the Moderate budget. The 2024 method sums XP directly; do not apply the 2014 monster-count multiplier.

**Intended pressure:** between Low and Moderate with the cargo-focused minion behavior and morale below. Minions have no official CR/XP rating; 200 XP is a budgeting allowance, not a certified rating for the homebrew variant. Five enemy turns and Korok's Pack Tactics still matter at level 1. Assume three PCs with normal starting resources and no added disease penalties. Reassess if they arrive depleted; do not infer their builds or access to healing.

Use printed average damage for the three official profiles to reduce ordinary damage swings; resolve critical hits normally. Korok's mace deals 5 damage, versus a bandit's scimitar dealing 4. His strength comes primarily from 32 HP and Pack Tactics, rather than a large damage spike or Multiattack.

### Minions — Campaign Homebrew

These are **modified Bandits**, not an official 5e minion statblock. Use the embedded Bandit profile with only these overrides:

- **HP 1.** Any damage reduces a recruit to 0 HP. No special immunity to damage on a successful saving throw; apply spells and areas normally. A damaging hit ends their participation even if the fiction is flight or collapse rather than death.
- **Weapon damage 1.** A successful scimitar or light crossbow attack deals a fixed 1 damage of its normal type. A critical hit still deals 1; this is an explicit homebrew exception. Attack bonuses and ranges remain unchanged.
- **No offensive Help.** Recruits do not use Help to grant attack advantage. Their physical presence can still enable Korok's official Pack Tactics; explain that visibly to players.
- **Cargo first.** On their first turn, recruits move toward unsecured supplies and use their action to unlash or lift a manageable bundle. Later they carry it away, Dodge, or Disengage. They attack only if a defender blocks them, and **at most one recruit makes an attack per round**. The other continues cargo work or withdraws. They do not pin down fleeing PCs or attack unconscious characters.
- **Brittle morale.** When either recruit falls or surrenders, the other flees or yields on their next turn. Both give up if Korok withdraws or falls.

Tell players that these two visibly frightened recruits are fragile opponents. Do not describe them as equally seasoned combatants and reveal the distinction only after damage.

## Terrain & Starting Positions — Tentative

Use a roughly **60-by-40-foot** area with a 15-foot-wide road, a shallow roadside ditch, scattered scrub, and a damaged culvert. There is no damaging fall or compulsory jump. The wagon stops about 20 feet short of the obstruction; PCs choose where they stand during the demand.

- **Korok:** in the road by the obstruction, about 20 feet ahead of the wagon. He is visible and reachable in one ordinary move from the wagon's front.
- **Carl and the other bandit:** one on each roadside, about 15 feet from Korok and 20–30 feet from the wagon. They step into view during the demand. Neither begins behind the PCs.
- **Two recruits:** visible near the roadside, about 30 feet from the wagon. They must move into reach of the cargo to steal it; they do not begin holding hostages or supplies.
- **Cover and movement:** substantial wagon sides and broken masonry can give half cover (+2 AC and Dexterity saves) when they actually block a shot. Scrub alone does not grant cover. Mark the shallow ditch as difficult terrain; keep the road and the route back open.

**Warning signs:** a freshly dragged branch, footprints beside the culvert, or movement in the scrub. A PC checking the road notices an actionable sign without a roll. An optional **DC 10 Wisdom (Perception)** check identifies the side positions before the bandits reveal themselves; this DC is a proposed encounter ruling. Failure does not force surprise or eliminate retreat. Moving the loose branch takes an action without a check if unopposed; moving the wagon through still requires someone to manage the team.

## Running the Encounter

1. **False inspection and choice.** Korok claims to represent the New Ostium city guard and demands access to inspect the wagon. Describe the outfit and weapon clues; use Darian's warning if needed. If allowed to inspect, Korok calls food, medicine, and coin “contraband” and orders their seizure; pause for PC responses before goods are removed. If exposed, he may abandon the disguise and demand the same cargo openly. Carl's attention repeatedly returns to the medicine. A credible limited offer, demonstrated resolve, or bypass can resolve the scene without combat; roll only if the reaction is uncertain. Suggested negotiation check: **DC 10 Charisma (Persuasion or Intimidation)**, a proposed ruling. Failure means Korok rejects those terms, not that initiative automatically begins.
2. **First combat round.** Roll initiative normally. Korok closes with a visible defender; Carl and the other bandit move to threaten access to the cargo, spreading pressure across available defenders. Recruits start cargo work. Enemies do not open with a coordinated crossbow volley against one PC.
3. **Following rounds.** Korok uses his mace; if an active ally is beside his target, apply Pack Tactics. Signal the tactic: he calls for someone to crowd that defender. PCs can separate them, defeat the ally, use cover, or withdraw. Bandits fight nearby threats or guard supplies; recruits follow their limited behavior above. No off-map reinforcements arrive.
4. **Morale and a second offer.** Carl yields if reduced to **5 HP or fewer**, or if a PC credibly offers a peaceful route to help his family. The unnamed bandit withdraws when Carl yields or Korok orders retreat. Korok calls for a truce or withdraws at **16 HP or fewer**, or when both full-strength bandits stop fighting. These are declared encounter behaviors, not saving throws or statblock powers; movement, Disengage, and opportunity attacks still work normally.
5. **A PC falls.** Korok calls a halt and offers to take a limited share of cargo in exchange for ending the fight. Attacks stop if the PCs accept; do not require a check to make that agreement. Enemies do not attack downed PCs. If fighting continues, the attackers prioritize cargo and escape. Death saves and stabilization still require normal resolution; a truce does not automatically heal anyone.

**Easier with the same roster:** if the PCs arrive depleted, have both recruits flee as soon as weapons are drawn. The three full-strength enemies remain, with a 150 XP baseline. Keep Korok's published statistics and announce his willingness to accept terms early. Do not add damage or reinforcements to compensate for good PC tactics.

It never requires Carl's death or a successful single search check. His words, hesitation, and letter provide separate routes to his family's circumstances. Neither he nor Korok acquires knowledge of Frigid Woe or an antidote through this encounter.

If using XP advancement, use **200 XP for overcoming the whole encounter**, including a negotiated or clever resolution; this is a campaign award using the conservative minion allowance. Divide among the three PCs (66 each, with 2 XP left for the table's rounding convention). If using milestones, retain the table's existing practice.
