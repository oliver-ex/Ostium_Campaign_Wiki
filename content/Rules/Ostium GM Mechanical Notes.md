# Ostium Mechanical Notes


## Design Priorities

- Use social and exploration pressure as often as combat.
- Old Ostium should remain inhabited; Rot-afflicted people are not automatically adversaries.
- In New Ostium, violence should have legal, social, and evidentiary consequences.
- Use environments to make locations active rather than treating every danger as an enemy stat block.
- Scale opposition using the chosen system's encounter design and actual attendance; Daggerheart tiers do not map directly to D&D levels.

## Rot Pressure

Until a dedicated Rot subsystem is designed, choose consequences that fit the current fiction:

- unsettling perception;
- intrusive connection;
- distorted attention;
- Stress (Daggerheart only);
- temporary separation from the group;
- an unwanted but potentially useful insight;
- exposure that becomes a later medical/story problem.

Do not use the Rot as a generic excuse to remove player agency.

## Faction Conflict

Daggerheart social scenes can carry just as much weight as fights. Faction leaders can be represented as Social or Leader adversaries when a formal mechanical contest helps, but ordinary conversation does not require a stat block.

## Daggerheart — Act I

Most starting scenes are Tier 1. Ulroc and Indra have Tier 2 blocks because a direct confrontation with either is intended to feel above the opening party's normal weight and is more likely to matter later.

## Daggerheart — Act II–III

Rebuild or scale key stat blocks when the party reaches them. The campaign outline deliberately avoids locking the final Heart/Rot King conflict to a tier before actual advancement is known.
