# Session Prep — The Road to Ostium

> Established premise comes from the GM's task. All new staging, dialogue, incidental characters, and possible consequences below are **Tentative** improvisation prompts. They become events only through play.

## Session Goals

- Give the party actionable routes to an antidote for [[Creatures and Afflictions/Frigid Woe|Frigid Woe]].
- Distinguish their illness from the Rot without defining new disease mechanics.
- Show unequal access to resources through particular people and practical decisions.
- Offer combat, negotiation, retreat, and investigation without prescribing outcomes.
- Let the party choose its next direction; the city arc and northern journey need not run in a fixed order.

## Opening Situation

The PCs contracted Frigid Woe in an earlier adventure and travel toward [[Locations/Ostium|Ostium]] aboard [[Characters/NPCs/Darian Auir|Darian Auir]]'s caravan. Its food, medical supplies, and gold attract an ambush ordered by [[Factions/The Shattered Axe|The Shattered Axe]], led by [[Characters/NPCs/Korok Grund|Korok Grund]], with [[Characters/NPCs/Carl Marsh|Carl Marsh]] among the attackers.

**Before play — Needs decision:** confirm attendees and party size; prior adventure recap; symptoms and prior mechanical rulings; exact remaining urgency; caravan/bandit numbers; courier cost/time and northern travel time; antidote dose requirements; session number/date. Do not infer these from the roster or invent a disease clock. Do not assume all eight PCs attend.

**Continuity:** this session is the new immediate opening, before or alongside the existing [[Session Logs/Session 1 - The Prodigal Son Prep|Session 1 - The Prodigal Son Prep]]. Keep that prep and [[Storylines/Adventures/The Prodigal Son|The Prodigal Son]] available as later or optional hooks; neither has become an accomplished event. Do not force the party to accept Egbert's job to access care.

## Opening Narration — Tentative

“The wagon creaks toward Ostium. Beyond the roadside rise the damaged outlines of the old city, with steadier roofs and walls beyond. Darian watches the road more than the skyline. Beneath the wagon's canvas sit food, medical supplies, and coin. You have come looking for help. Somewhere ahead is a city full of people doing the same.”

Invite each player to describe how their **already established** illness affects the journey. Supply no new symptom or infectious effect.

## Likely Scenes

These are movable situations. Follow the party's route, skip bypassed scenes, and carry essential leads to plausible alternate sources.

### 1. Caravan Journey and Ambush — 30–50 minutes

**Dramatic question:** can travelers and supplies reach the city without a costly fight?

- **Darian interaction:** ask why the PCs are going to Ostium; let them ask about the cargo, ordinary road risks, or city approaches. Disease expertise is not established for him.
- **Road details — Tentative:** patched ruts, an old drainage ditch, broken roadside masonry, cart tracks, a narrow bend beside scrub. No new named settlement or canon map required.
- **Ambush site — Tentative:** a damaged culvert narrows the road; cover on either side permits intimidation and withdrawal. A wagon can stop before the pinch point; keep a route back and space for passengers to dismount.
- **Notice / avoidance:** visible churned tracks, unusual silence, movement behind cover, or a partly shifted obstruction. Observant precautions reveal warning signs; a roll can improve position or certainty, not determine whether any choice exists.
- **Bandit demand:** leave the food, medicine, and gold. Korok wants a haul, not a massacre. Carl may hesitate near civilians.
- **Tactics:** threaten movement and cargo, seek cover, offer terms, concentrate pressure rather than surround every escape. Korok may withdraw or surrender when losses outweigh gain.
- **Alternatives:** parley, offer a limited share, convince attackers the cost is too high, retreat before entering the choke point, scout an alternate passage, or cooperate temporarily while seeking recovery later.
- **Letter:** [[Attachments/Carl Marsh - Unsent Letter|Carl Marsh - Unsent Letter]] can be noticed with Carl's belongings, voluntarily offered, or dropped during retreat. Discovery does not require killing him.

| Outcome | Immediate result — Tentative | Continuing lead |
|---|---|---|
| PCs win | Secure cargo; decide treatment of wounded and captives | Reach Ostium; Carl or letter offers human context |
| PCs lose / surrender | Bandits take accessible cargo; bargain over passengers and basic necessities | Survivors can continue toward care; no automatic execution or campaign-ending capture |
| PCs flee | Safety or separation at the cost of abandoned cargo | Regroup and choose city or north; Darian's losses may matter later |
| PCs negotiate | Agreed exchange or stand-down, with terms chosen at table | Trust and faction reactions follow how terms are honored |
| PCs avoid the site | Ambush can be bypassed; no automatic relocation of the same fight | Human context may emerge later if relevant, rather than forcing Carl into their path |

### 2. The Human Cost — 10–20 minutes, if encountered

**Dramatic question:** what responsibility follows understanding someone's desperation?

- Carl needs money for food for his wife and medicine for his daughter in Old Ostium. The diagnosis is unspecified.
- Routes to discovery: conversation, surrender, his letter, reluctance to harm civilians, or another survivor's testimony. Testimony about him must remain limited to what the witness could plausibly know.
- Darian may sympathize while pointing out that stolen medicine was intended for other patients. Let disagreement stand without a forced moral answer.
- Captive options include disarmament, negotiated release, restitution, escort, or handing over to a relevant authority. No instant universal sentence; decide jurisdiction and response from the fiction.
- Carl can ask for help, but the PCs need not solve his family's problems before seeking their own treatment.
- One desperate recruit does not make leadership benevolent. Preserve existing faction coercion and cruelty alongside this individual motive.

### 3. Arrival in Ostium — 15–25 minutes

**Dramatic question:** where will the party seek help, and whom will the city welcome?

**Old Ostium approach — Tentative narration:** “Broken masonry forces the wagon to slow. People still carry water and repair doors beneath walls that have lost their upper floors. Outside a crowded treatment room, someone argues over which supplies can wait until tomorrow.”

- Damaged, partly collapsed streets; occupied homes and people maintaining precarious services.
- Disease, poverty, deteriorating infrastructure, overwhelmed care, and suspicion of authorities and outsiders.
- Rot is a major health concern. Use existing [[Locations/Old Ostium|Old Ostium]] material; do not reveal its true nature.

**New Ostium approach — Tentative narration:** “The roadway grows firmer beneath the wheels. Roofs are maintained; deliveries move with purpose. A person watching the arriving travelers steps back at the sight of illness. The question is not yet what you need, but whether you will be allowed close enough to ask.”

- Greater stability and wealth, alongside contamination anxiety, prejudice, and unequal medical access.
- A concerned attendant or guard may ask for assessment, distance, or a sponsor. This is a possible complication, not a new citywide law.
- Social fear does not establish actual contagion or justify every restriction. Permit advocacy, medical explanation, an escort, legitimate alternate access, or asking Shanda to meet them.
- Let players choose either city, a contact, an alternative healer, or immediate travel north. Do not force the hospital-first route.

### 4. The Old Hospital Quarter — 20–35 minutes, if visited

Use [[Locations/Old Ostium Hospital District|the existing Hospital District]], not a second district. [[Characters/NPCs/Arla Gerrin|Arla Gerrin]] works alongside its existing people and pressures.

**Tentative scene:** crowded benches, washed cloth drying near a window, a volunteer counting bandages, a patient helping another reach a seat. The people here contribute rather than existing only as scenery.

- Minor encounters: a road-injury patient needs a steady hand while a dressing is changed; a caregiver asks whether a delivery arrived; an exhausted volunteer asks for water-carrying help.
- Arla examines the party using symptoms already established in play. She recognizes this is not the Rot and identifies or strongly suspects Frigid Woe.
- **Freely available:** distinction from the Rot, disease identification/suspicion, her lack of antidote, and Shanda as a useful lead in New Ostium.
- **Further questions:** why she suspects that diagnosis, what her actual experience permits her to say, and how to reach Shanda. If symptoms/prior records are missing, flag the diagnostic specifics rather than fabricate them.
- **Optional help:** carry supplies or settle a queue dispute. She still gives the lead if they decline.
- **Complication — Tentative:** shortages interrupt her work; assistance improves relationships or buys time for a longer conversation, rather than unlocking the only clue.
- Arla is not established as knowing ancient Auren history or exact Mistbreak stock levels.

### 5. Shanda Karth's Apothecary — 25–40 minutes, if visited

**Dramatic question:** how will the party secure treatment without treating other lives as disposable?

**Tentative setting:** an orderly counter, carefully measured stock, a workroom beyond. Do not assume the two vials are openly displayed or trivially accessible; establish layout and precautions fairly before theft decisions.

[[Characters/NPCs/Shanda Karth|Shanda Karth]] holds **two vials owned by [[Characters/NPCs/Egbert Lusat|Egbert Lusat]]**, one reserved for each of his two sons as a precaution following northern reports; his wife [[Characters/NPCs/Tara Lusat|Tara Lusat]] died from the Rot of Old Ostium. Shanda cannot independently sell them; Egbert may authorize a negotiated sale or transfer. She knows a supply recently arrived in [[Locations/Mistbreak|Mistbreak]] and can potentially arrange a paid courier.

| PC approach | Usable response / branch — Tentative |
|---|---|
| Offer money | Explain client ownership; offer to ask Egbert about sale of one or both, or discuss courier expenses |
| Appeal to sympathy | Acknowledge their danger; share the northern lead and practical assistance |
| Attempt persuasion | Improve help, introductions, or logistics; a roll does not erase the reservation |
| Ask about the benefactor | Name Egbert Lusat; explain his loss and family, and offer an optional introduction |
| Offer a favor | Offer an introduction to discuss the existing Prodigal Son situation; reward and success terms are negotiated, not a new compulsory quest |
| Hire a courier | Set cost, route, timing, collection terms, and contingencies openly before agreement; do not promise guaranteed stock |
| Attempt theft | Establish access, witnesses, and stakes; adjudicate from fiction; two vials do not establish sufficient treatment for everyone |
| Travel north | Give Mistbreak as the concrete destination; discuss existing travel options without a gating check |

If they bypass Arla, Shanda can be the first medical lead and explain her stock. If they bypass Shanda, use a plausible healer/trade contact to point north as a **Tentative alternate source**, with their actual knowledge established at the table. No exact price or impossible delay invented solely to block a choice.

### Optional Scene: Meeting the Benefactor — 10–20 minutes

**Entry:** Shanda offers an introduction to [[Characters/NPCs/Egbert Lusat|Egbert Lusat]] if the PCs want one. They may instead purchase help with a courier or go directly to Mistbreak. No check or quest acceptance is required to hear the options.

**Setting — Tentative:** a comfortable but practical merchant's office or sitting room in New Ostium, with trade papers on a desk and chairs offered for tired visitors. Exact address and household details are not established. The antidote remains at Shanda's, not in a second cache here.

**Initial reaction:** courteous concern. He lets them explain their predicament rather than treating their need as bargaining leverage. If they ask why he holds the medicine, he explains briefly that his wife [[Characters/NPCs/Tara Lusat|Tara Lusat]] died from the Rot of Old Ostium, and he purchased one extraordinarily expensive vial for each of his two sons after reports from the north. Neither son is established as infected with Frigid Woe.

**Natural quest bridge:** “My son Taran is helping people in the Hospital District. I want him safe, but he will not leave those who depend on him.” He may offer the two vials as negotiated compensation for resolving the existing [[Storylines/Adventures/The Prodigal Son|Prodigal Son]] situation. Keep Taran's genuine infrastructure duty and the possibility of a safety/settlement compromise. His current Rot danger recalls the illness that killed his mother, Tara; it remains distinct from the PCs' Frigid Woe.

**Questions to prepare:**

- Why reserve medicine when neither son is known to have Frigid Woe? Losing Tara to the Rot makes him determined to protect his family against other illnesses as well; the Frigid Woe purchase followed reports from the north.
- Can we buy one or both? He may accept substantial payment that addresses replacement expense and risk; exact amount TBD.
- Must we complete the quest first? No. Credible assurances, collateral, immediate assistance, or a replacement plan may justify an advance.
- What would count as helping Taran? Agree terms against the existing safety/duty dilemma; do not silently redefine success as abduction or forced extraction.
- Can we earn money another way, arrange a courier, or travel ourselves? Yes; the northern lead and other work remain available.
- Will the vials cure Taran's Rot or everyone in the party? No such effect or dosing rule is established.

**Short dialogue — Tentative prompts:**

- “The Rot took my wife, Tara. It was a different illness, but I know the fear of losing someone.”
- “There are two vials, one for each of my sons. Giving them up is not only a question of money.”
- “Tell me what you can offer, and what you need now. Perhaps we can protect your people and mine.”
- “Taran will not abandon the Hospital District. If you can help us find a way to keep him safe, we can discuss the medicine as payment.”

**Outcomes:** negotiate reward on agreed quest success; sell one or both for substantial gold; authorize immediate release against adequate assurances/collateral/assistance; agree a replacement/courier plan; or part courteously while the PCs pursue other work, research, or direct northern travel. Theft remains their choice with consequences. Apply [[Characters/NPCs/Egbert Lusat#Antidote Negotiation — GM Quick Reference|his priorities]] without reducing the meeting to one Persuasion roll. No new deadline or guaranteed replacement stock.

## Daggerheart — Fear Opportunities / Consequences — Tentative

Use only consequences supported by the developing fiction: a wagon wheel catches in a rut, a supply crate is exposed, frightened animals need calming, a conversation draws a worried crowd, or a delivery interrupts negotiation. Telegraph meaningful danger and preserve responses.

Do not add disease damage, accelerate infection, declare contagion, remove the last lead, or spend Fear to predetermine surrender or guilt.

## Session Pacing

- **Essential revelations, wherever obtained:** distinguish illness from Rot; treatment exists; Mistbreak is a viable lead; choose a next step. Reserve roughly 45–75 minutes for these decisions and conversations.
- **Requested opening situation:** journey/ambush, 30–50 minutes; avoidance or negotiation can shorten it. Human aftermath 10–20 minutes if encountered.
- **Optional:** arrival detail 15–25 minutes; Darian conversation, Carl's letter, hospital vignettes, and extended patron negotiation as interest allows.
- **Likely city scenes:** Arla 20–35 minutes and Shanda 25–40 minutes, only if visited. These timings overlap the essential-revelation budget rather than adding another required block.
- **Closing decision:** 10–20 minutes. Aim for a flexible 2–3 hour core, with breaks and expanded RP as appropriate.
- **Optional benefactor meeting:** 10–20 minutes if chosen; shorten another conversation or defer it to keep pacing flexible.
- **Defer:** Torney exploration, Auren research, northern travel, or existing Prodigal Son scenes unless chosen now.
- If short on time, summarize travel and use one well-informed contact. Do not end before players understand at least one actionable route forward.
